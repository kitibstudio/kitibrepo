§4.9 — SLD Symbol Library, Uptime Tiers Continued

Tier 3 — two independent paths, one late merge

The redundancy moves upstream: instead of one utility/generator/ATS front end with N+1 UPS bolted on (Tier 2), there are two complete, physically separate chains — each with its own utility feed, generator, ATS, and UPS. They meet for the first time at a Static Transfer Switch immediately before the load.

Path A and Path B share no equipment until STS1 — either can be fully de-energized (Q1, ATS, UPS, everything) with zero impact on PDU-1
Why this — and not Tier 2 with a fatter B1 — is Tier 3: in the Tier 2 example, Utility, Q1, GA, and ATS1 were single points shared by both UPS branches; taking Q1 or ATS1 out for maintenance dropped both UPS modules at once. Here, Path A and Path B don't share a single component: separate utility feed, separate breaker, separate generator, separate ATS, separate UPS. STS1 is deliberately the only shared device, and it's built to switch loads between sources without an interruption — that's what "concurrently maintainable" actually requires, not just spare capacity.
The diff across all three tiers is legible at a glance in a way three separate hand-drawn diagrams never are: Tier 1 → Tier 2 adds a second UPS branch off the same B1. Tier 2 → Tier 3 doesn't add a device to the existing chain at all — it duplicates the entire chain and moves the merge point from a shared busbar (B1) to a purpose-built transfer device (STS1) right at the load. That's a structural change a snapshot diff would show as "two new independent subtrees, old B2 branch removed," not a scattering of moved boxes.
